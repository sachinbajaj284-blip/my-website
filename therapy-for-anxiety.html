What anxiety counselling can help with

You don’t need a diagnosis to book, and you don’t need to be in crisis. These are the forms anxiety most often takes when people reach out:

🧠
Worry that won’t stop

A mind that jumps from one “what if” to the next and never quite settles, even when nothing’s wrong.

💓
The physical side

Racing heart, tight chest, a knotted stomach, restlessness, muscles that stay tense even at rest.

💨
Panic attacks

Sudden surges of fear with pounding heart and breathlessness — frightening, and very workable with support.

😴
Sleep & focus

Lying awake with a racing mind, trouble concentrating, feeling on edge and exhausted at once.

🚫
Avoidance

Turning down things you want to do, or getting through them braced — the quiet cost anxiety adds up over time.

📚
Exam & performance anxiety

Blanking despite preparing, dread before presentations, panic that peaks at exactly the wrong moment.

Frequently asked questions

Can counselling actually help my anxiety?

Yes. Anxiety is highly manageable — counselling helps you understand what keeps it going and build skills to lower it, from how you respond to the physical symptoms to gently reversing avoidance. For many people the change is large.

Is the anxiety check a diagnosis?

No. The GAD-7 is a brief, well-known reflection scale that tells you roughly how heavy the last couple of weeks have been. It isn’t a diagnosis — only a qualified professional can make one — and your answers aren’t stored or sent anywhere.

Do I need medication for anxiety?

Not necessarily. Many people find counselling, skills and lifestyle changes are enough, especially for mild to moderate anxiety. If anxiety is severe, a psychiatrist can assess whether medication would help — and your counsellor will tell you honestly if that’s the direction to go.

Will anyone find out I’m getting help for anxiety?

No. Nothing is shared with your parents, school, college or employer — no report, no summary, not even confirmation that you attended. A first name is enough to book.
