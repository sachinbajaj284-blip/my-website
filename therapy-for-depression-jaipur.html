What counselling for low mood can help with in Jaipur

You don’t need a diagnosis or a reason that looks big enough from outside. These are the things people bring when low mood has been weighing on them:

🌁
Loss of interest

Things you used to enjoy going flat — hobbies, people, plans. Often the earliest and most honest sign.

😶
Exhaustion & sleep

Tiredness sleep doesn’t fix, mornings getting harder, everything taking more effort than it should.

🧠
The harsh inner voice

Guilt, feeling like a burden, a running self-criticism you’d never accept aimed at anyone else.

😓
Withdrawing

Pulling back from friends and family without quite deciding to, and the loneliness that follows.

🌑
Numbness, not just sadness

Feeling flat or empty rather than tearful — the version of low mood that’s easiest to miss and dismiss.

🫀
Feeling stuck

Drift after a setback, a loss or a change — where low mood and feeling directionless feed each other.

What the pressure looks like here

Nowhere else in India has quite Rajasthan’s relationship with competitive exams. Kota is two hundred and fifty kilometres up the road, it’s the national centre of entrance coaching, and the culture around it reaches into every family in the state. Most Jaipur students either go to Kota, come back from Kota, or grow up being measured against a cousin who did.

That creates a particular trap. The route is so well established socially that stepping off it looks like failing rather than choosing. A student who’s struggling has no way to say so that doesn’t sound like giving up. So they don’t say it. They carry on, often through a drop year, sometimes two.

The drop year deserves its own paragraph. People outside it badly underestimate how isolating it is. School friends have gone off to colleges and new lives, your days have no shape except studying, and every conversation with a relative turns into a progress review you didn’t ask for.

Frequently asked questions

I am in a drop year and it is going badly. Is that worth a session?

Yes, and it is one of the most common reasons students in Rajasthan book. The drop year is isolating in a way people outside it underestimate, and most of the difficulty is that structure and company have disappeared at the same time, not that you lack discipline.

I want to stop preparing but can’t say so at home. Can counselling help?

That is squarely the kind of thing a session is for. It usually helps to work out what you actually want before working out how to say it — those are two separate problems, and trying to solve them at once is what makes it feel unsayable.

Will my parents or my coaching institute be told?

No. Nothing goes to your family, your institute or anyone else, and you can book using a first name only. If you’re under 18, a parent does need to consent to a paid session, but what you discuss stays confidential.

Is the session in Hindi?

Yes if you want it to be. Hindi or English, whichever you think in — and for most students in Rajasthan the difficult things are much easier to reach in Hindi.

Can counselling help with depression?

Often, yes — especially for mild to moderate low mood, where counselling, support and small changes make a real difference. More severe depression sometimes needs medication alongside, and a counsellor will tell you honestly.

I feel numb rather than sad. Is that still worth talking about?

Yes. Loss of feeling — not enjoying anything, feeling flat or empty — is a core feature of depression and is often missed. It’s very much worth talking through.
