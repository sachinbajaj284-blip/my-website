How to Become a Software Engineer in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Software engineering is the career that quietly runs the rest of them — banking, healthcare, entertainment, government, all built on code. It is also one of the most stream-forgiving, merit-driven careers in India: the door opens widest from Science, but skill matters more than pedigree once you are inside.

What a software engineer actually does

A software engineer designs, builds and maintains the programs that run on phones, websites, servers and machines. The day-to-day is less “typing fast in a dark room” and more solving problems in small steps: understanding what a feature needs to do, breaking it into pieces, writing and testing code, fixing what breaks, and collaborating with designers, testers and product managers. Specialisations include front-end (what users see), back-end (servers and data), mobile, data engineering, DevOps and, increasingly, AI/ML.

The path after Class 10

Choose Science with Mathematics (PCM) if you can — a B.Tech in Computer Science requires it. But this is not a locked gate: BCA and B.Sc Computer Science welcome Commerce students, and many excellent developers came in sideways. What genuinely predicts success here is comfort with logic and maths, and the patience to debug something that refuses to work. If those describe you, the stream is a formality.

The path after Class 12

  • B.Tech / B.E. in Computer Science or IT — the mainstream route, via JEE Main & Advanced (IITs, NITs, IIITs), state CETs (MHT-CET, KCET, etc.), or private-university exams (BITSAT, VITEEE, SRMJEEE).
  • BCA → MCA — a strong, less exam-intense route, increasingly accepted via CUET at central universities.
  • B.Sc Computer Science / IT — cheaper, flexible, and a solid base for a Master’s or direct entry with a good portfolio.
  • Self-taught + degree in anything — unusual but real: consistent projects, open-source contributions and internships can open doors even from a non-CS degree.

Pay in software has an unusually wide spread. The gap between a service-company and a top-product role for the same experience can be 4–5x — which is exactly why skill, projects and interview preparation matter more here than in most careers.

A day in the life

A typical day: a short morning stand-up to align with the team; a few hours of focused coding on a feature or bug; a code review or two (reading and improving teammates’ work); testing and deployment; and some collaboration with product or design. Good engineers protect long blocks of uninterrupted “deep work” time — the craft rewards concentration.

Is this career right for you?

Software tends to suit students whose interests lean Investigative and Realistic in the Holland RIASEC model — people who enjoy figuring out how things work and building tangible results. You do not need to be a maths genius, but you do need to enjoy problem-solving that fails before it succeeds. If debugging a stubborn problem sounds satisfying rather than exhausting, that is a strong signal.

Key takeaways
  • PCM is the smoothest path, but BCA/B.Sc routes keep the career open to Commerce students too.
  • A top college helps with the first job; skill and projects decide the rest of the career.
  • Salaries have a very wide spread — preparation and continuous learning move you up the range.
  • Best-fit interests: Investigative + Realistic. Enjoy problem-solving that fails before it works.
