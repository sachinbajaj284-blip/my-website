What it actually knows

It asks. You just reply.

A general-purpose chatbot answers whatever you happen to ask, from whatever it read on the internet. Buddy asks you what it needs to know, one question per message, and answers from a fixed library our counsellors maintain — saying so plainly when it doesn’t know.

  • One question at a time, never a form — it asks what actually changes the answer: which class, which subjects, which state. It won’t make you fill anything in, and it won’t ask for your name, school or marks.
  • 97 careers and 51 government routes — the same career library behind our reports, with entry paths, realistic pay bands and what the work is actually like day to day.
  • The decision calendar, not just the destination — that dropping Maths in Class 11 quietly closes engineering and most economics routes, that Applied Maths isn't a universal substitute, that CA runs through ICAI rather than a college admission.
  • Your own scores, if you link them — your RIASEC interests, work anchors and Big Five from the free assessment, so the answer is about you rather than about students in general.
  • When to stop asking a machine — it can book you a session, and it hands over to a counsellor rather than trying to be one.

The limits, stated plainly

What it will not do

These aren't disclaimers. They're the reasons it's safe to give a sixteen-year-old.

  • It won't pretend to be a counsellor. Ask who you're talking to and it says it's an assistant. It will not do therapy, and it refers rather than pushes on.
  • It won't invent a cut-off. Our JoSAA rank data isn't published yet, so if you ask which college a rank gets you, it says it doesn't have that data instead of guessing. A student picks a college on that number.
  • It won't read your profile until you link it. A phone number isn't proof of identity. Your scores stay unreadable until you sign in and connect the number yourself — and you can disconnect it whenever you want.
  • It stops if someone is in trouble. Every message is screened before a reply is written. If it reads distress, no AI reply is composed at all: you get helpline numbers a person checked, and the conversation goes to a human.

Questions

Before you ask it anything

Is it free?

Asking Buddy is free. It can tell you what our reports and sessions cost and book one for you, but the conversation itself isn't a paid product.

Is this replacing your counsellors?

No, and it's built so it can't. It drafts and it answers; it doesn't sign anything. Every written report still goes to a qualified counsellor who edits and signs it before you see a word of it — the AI's draft is never what gets sent.

Can it see my assessment without me linking it?

No. Nothing it can do carries your identity — it's given an account only after you sign in and send the code yourself. Until then it will discuss careers all day and read nothing personal about you.

What if I'm not okay?

Say so. The conversation goes to a person, and you'll get numbers that are answered by trained people: Tele-MANAS 14416, KIRAN 1800-599-0019, AASRA +91 98204 66726. If it's an emergency, please call one of those now rather than message us.

Which languages does it understand?

English, Hindi, and the mix of the two most people actually type. Reply however is comfortable.

Ask it something you're stuck on

Stream choice, a course you've never heard of, whether a rank is worth repeating a year for.
