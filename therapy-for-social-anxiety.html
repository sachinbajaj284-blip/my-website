What counselling for social anxiety can help with

You don’t need a diagnosis to reach out. These are the forms social anxiety most often takes:

👁️
Fear of judgement

A constant sense of being watched and found wanting, long after the moment has passed.

🗣️
Speaking up & being seen

Dread of presentations, meetings, class, phone calls — anywhere attention might land on you.

😳
The physical tells

Blushing, shaking, a trembling voice or going blank — and the fear that everyone noticed.

🔁
Replaying it afterwards

Picking over every conversation for what you “got wrong”, sometimes for days.

🚪
Avoidance

Turning down invitations, staying quiet, or steering around people — and a life that keeps narrowing.

💼
The cost to study & work

Holding back in class or at work, so your ability never quite shows — and the frustration of that.

Frequently asked questions

Is social anxiety just shyness?

No. Shyness is a trait; social anxiety is a persistent fear of judgement strong enough to make you avoid things you actually want to do, and to cost you at work, in study and in relationships. That difference — the avoidance and the toll — is exactly what support helps with.

Can counselling help if I’m anxious about the session itself?

Yes, and it’s very common — the fact that it’s online, from your own space, with the option of voice or chat rather than video, takes a lot of that pressure off. Feeling nervous about reaching out is part of what you’re reaching out about, and that’s understood from the first minute.

Is the anxiety check a diagnosis?

No. The GAD-7 is a brief reflection scale, not a diagnosis, and your answers aren’t stored. Only a qualified professional can diagnose a social anxiety disorder; your counsellor will say so if that’s the direction.

Will anyone find out?

No. Nothing is shared with your parents, school, college or employer — no report, no summary, not even confirmation that you attended. A first name is enough to book.
