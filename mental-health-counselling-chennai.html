Talking to someone qualified in Chennai — without the awkwardness

What the pressure looks like here

Chennai starts the race early and runs it hard. NEET and the engineering entrance sit at the centre of most families’ plans, the big corporate colleges drill for rank from Class 11, and a long-standing belief that medicine or engineering are the only respectable outcomes narrows the field before a teenager has really looked up. For the students that suits, the system works. For the many simply routed into it, the strain builds quietly.

Two patterns come up again and again in sessions. One is the student who moved from a smaller town in Tamil Nadu for a college seat and knows almost no one — the loneliness of a shared hostel room does more damage than the syllabus ever does. The other is the young professional on the OMR IT corridor, a few years into a job, running on deadlines and a commute, who can’t work out when exhaustion became the baseline.

What is already available in Chennai, and what’s missing

Chennai is better served than most cities. The Institute of Mental Health at Kilpauk is one of the oldest and largest government psychiatric hospitals in the country, and SCARF India has decades of serious clinical and research work behind it. If you need a psychiatric assessment, or things are severe, those are the right places to go.

What neither is built for is the much larger group who aren’t ill but are struggling anyway — the student who can’t make themselves open the book, the aspirant on a second NEET attempt, the twenty-six-year-old who has stopped enjoying anything and can’t say why. Public psychiatry comes with waiting lists and a clinical threshold you have to cross first. Private therapy here runs ₹1,500 to ₹3,000 a session, which for a student usually means asking a parent, which for many means not going at all.

Sessions here happen online, so nobody sees you arrive, and a session is ₹499.

What this is, and what it isn’t

It’s counselling support, not treatment: confidential conversations about what you’re carrying and what might help. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to travel anywhere in Chennai for a session?

No. Sessions are online by video, voice or chat, so there’s no clinic to reach across the city and no waiting room to sit in. For most people in Chennai the traffic alone is a real barrier, and so is the worry of being seen.

Is this available in Tamil or Hindi?

Sessions run in English or Hindi, whichever you think in more easily. Many people in Chennai are most comfortable moving between English and their mother tongue, and you’re welcome to do that — what matters is that you can say what’s going on without translating it first.

My child is in a corporate college and always stressed. Is one session useful?

Yes, and one is a reasonable amount to start with. Forty-five minutes, online, at a slot you pick. A great deal of the distress in Chennai’s rank-driven colleges is about isolation and self-worth tied to marks rather than the syllabus itself, and that is workable in a single conversation.

Will my parents find out?

No. Nothing is reported to your parents, and you can book using a first name only. This is one of the most common questions we are asked in Chennai, and the answer has no caveats beyond the standard limit where there is serious risk to life.
