How to Become a Data Scientist in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Data science sits where mathematics, coding and business questions meet. If you like finding patterns, arguing from evidence and being the person who says “actually, the numbers say something different” — this may be your field. It is analytical work, and the maths is not optional.

What a data scientist actually does

A data scientist turns messy data into decisions. That means collecting and cleaning data, exploring it for patterns, building statistical and machine-learning models, and — the part people forget — explaining the findings clearly to people who are not technical. Related roles include data analyst (more reporting and dashboards), machine-learning engineer (more coding and deployment) and data engineer (building the pipelines that feed everything else).

The path after Class 10

Take Science with Mathematics (PCM) if you can — statistics, probability and linear algebra are the bedrock of this field, and getting comfortable with maths early pays off for years. Commerce-with-Maths students can also enter through Economics and Statistics routes. The honest filter here is simple: if maths feels like a wall rather than a puzzle, look carefully before committing, because it never goes away in this career.

The path after Class 12

  • B.Tech in CSE / Data Science / AI — via JEE or state/private entrance exams; the most common route today.
  • B.Sc in Statistics, Mathematics, Economics or Physics — a strong analytical base, usually followed by an M.Sc or PG in Data Science.
  • BCA / B.Sc Computer Science → specialised Master’s — a coding-first route into the field.
  • Integrated Statistics / Data Science programmes — ISI Kolkata, IITs, IISc and central universities (via CUET and dedicated exams) offer rigorous, respected degrees.

Core skills to build

  • Statistics & probability — the true foundation; more important than any single tool.
  • Programming — Python (and SQL) are the working languages of the field.
  • Machine learning — how models learn, and just as importantly, when to trust them.
  • Communication — turning a chart into a decision a manager can act on.

Is this career right for you?

Data science strongly suits Investigative interests in the Holland RIASEC model, often paired with Conventional (a liking for structure and precision). The best data scientists are curious, sceptical and patient with detail — they enjoy the moment a pattern finally reveals itself in the noise. If you like being right for reasons you can prove, this is a good sign.

Key takeaways
  • Maths is the core, not a side-skill — PCM (or Commerce-with-Maths) is the sensible base.
  • Many routes work: B.Tech, B.Sc Statistics/Economics, or a specialised Master’s.
  • Statistics + Python + clear communication beat any single certificate.
  • Best-fit interests: Investigative + Conventional. You enjoy proving things with evidence.
