What ADHD counselling support can help with

A diagnosis is a separate, clinical step — but you don’t need one to get support with how this shows up day to day:

🧩
Focus & follow-through

Starting tasks, finishing them, and the gap between what you mean to do and what actually gets done.

🗂️
Organisation & time

Deadlines, planning, losing track of time, and systems that keep sliding no matter how hard you try.

⚡
Impulsivity & restlessness

Acting or speaking before you mean to, a mind that won’t sit still, difficulty waiting.

😔
The emotional toll

Years of “not living up to potential”, low self-esteem, and feeling like you’re always playing catch-up.

🎢
Emotional intensity

Feelings that hit hard and fast, frustration that spikes, rejection that stings more than it should.

😴
Overwhelm & shutdown

Too many tabs open in your head, then freezing — and the exhaustion of masking it all day.

Frequently asked questions

Can you diagnose my ADHD?

No — an ADHD diagnosis is a formal clinical assessment, usually by a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist qualified to do it, and this is non-diagnostic counselling support. What we can do is help you with the day-to-day impact and the emotional side, and, if a formal assessment looks worthwhile, point you toward getting one.

Can counselling help ADHD without medication?

It can help a lot with the practical and emotional side — building systems that actually stick, working with your attention rather than against it, and undoing years of harsh self-judgement. For many people medication (prescribed by a psychiatrist) also helps significantly; the two work well together, and we’ll be honest about when an assessment is worth pursuing.

I was told I’m just lazy or careless. Is that true?

Almost certainly not. The hallmark of ADHD is that effort and outcome don’t match — you can be trying hard and still struggle with things others find easy. A great deal of the work here is separating the condition from the character you were unfairly handed.

Will anyone find out?

No. Nothing is shared with your parents, school, college or employer — no report, no summary, not even confirmation that you attended. A first name is enough to book.
