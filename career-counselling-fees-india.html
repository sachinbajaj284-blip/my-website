Career Counselling Fees in India (2026): What to Expect and What to Question

By the Lume Live Counselling Team, M.Sc Clinical Psychology · Updated July 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

Career counselling fees in India vary enormously: single online sessions are advertised from a few hundred rupees to ₹2,000-₹3,000 with independent counsellors, while multi-session packages at national platforms commonly run from several thousand to ₹25,000 or more. Because the industry is unregulated, price correlates poorly with quality — qualification and assessment methodology are the real signals.

One honesty note before we go further: there is no official fee survey for this industry, so every number above describes commonly advertised ranges, not verified market data. That vagueness is itself the story — in a market this opaque, the smartest question isn't "what's the right price?" but "what am I actually getting for it?" Daam se pehle, saaman poochiye.

Why the prices vary so wildly

  • Marketing costs wear a counselling costume. National platforms spend heavily on ads and franchise networks — and those costs live inside your fee. You may be paying ₹15,000 for ₹4,000 of counselling and ₹11,000 of customer acquisition.
  • Packaging inflates totals. "Comprehensive career solutions" bundle sessions you may not need, reports of varying value, and vague extras ("lifetime dashboard access") that cost the provider nothing.
  • Information asymmetry rewards confident pricing. Families can't easily judge counselling quality before buying, so some services price high specifically because a high price signals credibility. It's the one industry trick worth naming out loud: expensive is a marketing strategy, not a qualification.

What your fee should actually include

Whatever the price point, a legitimate career counselling service includes four things. Treat this as your checklist:

  1. A validated, named assessment. A framework you can independently look up — Holland RIASEC, VARK, a values inventory. If the test is "proprietary," unnamed, or fingerprint-based (DMIT), the assessment portion of your fee is buying decoration.
  2. A real 1:1 session with a named, qualified person. Not "one of our certified experts" — a specific human whose psychology or counselling degree you can verify. The session should be mostly you talking.
  3. A written output you keep. A report or roadmap with specific next steps — subjects, exams, options, timelines. If the deliverable is a motivational conversation and a brochure, you bought a sales meeting.
  4. Transparent follow-up pricing. Know before session one what session two costs and whether it's optional. Most families genuinely need one to three sessions; be wary of anyone whose model requires more.

Red flags that cost more than money

  • The "free consultation" funnel — a no-cost call that turns out to be a package pitch, with counselling starting only after ₹10,000+ changes hands.
  • Pressure pricing — "this discount is valid only today." Real counsellors don't sell like gym memberships.
  • Guaranteed outcomes — "100% right career guaranteed" is a promise no ethical professional can make about a human being's future.
  • The commission question nobody asks — some "counsellors" earn referral commissions from colleges and coaching centres. Their advice tilts toward whoever pays them. Ask directly: "Do you receive any payment from institutions you recommend?"

As a counsellor, this is the part that bothers me most: the families with the least money to waste are the likeliest to be overcharged, because they assume the ₹20,000 service must be serious. Meanwhile the thing that actually produces clarity — a qualified person, a valid assessment, an honest conversation — has never been expensive to deliver.

Frequently asked questions

How much does career counselling cost in India?

Advertised prices span a wide range: single online sessions from a few hundred rupees to ₹2,000-₹3,000 with independent counsellors, and multi-session packages at large national platforms commonly advertised anywhere from several thousand to ₹25,000 or more. Price correlates poorly with quality because the industry is unregulated — the counsellor's qualification and assessment methodology matter far more than the fee.

Why is there such a huge price difference between services?

Three reasons: brand and marketing costs (national platforms spend heavily on advertising, which fees must recover), packaging (bundles of sessions, reports and "lifetime access" inflate totals), and information asymmetry — families cannot easily judge quality, so some services price high specifically to signal credibility they may not have. A high fee funds marketing as often as it funds expertise.

What should be included in a career counselling fee?

At minimum: a validated psychometric assessment (named framework you can look up, such as Holland RIASEC — not DMIT), a genuine 1:1 session with a named and qualified counsellor, a written report or roadmap you keep, and clarity upfront about what follow-ups cost. If any of these four is missing or vague, ask why before paying.

Is expensive career counselling better?

Not reliably. In an unregulated market, price signals marketing budget as much as expertise. A ₹15,000 package delivered by an unnamed "expert" with a proprietary test is worse value than a modestly priced session with a named M.Sc psychologist using validated tools. Judge the qualification, the assessment and the transparency — then consider the price.
