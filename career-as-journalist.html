How to Become a Journalist in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Journalism is finding the truth and telling it clearly — across print, TV and, increasingly, digital. It rewards curiosity, nerve and a way with words, and it has changed hugely with the internet, which is both its challenge and its opportunity.

💼 What a journalist actually does

A journalist reports, investigates, writes and presents news and stories — interviewing people, verifying facts and explaining what’s happening and why. The field spans print, television, radio, and a fast-growing digital world of online publications, video, podcasts and data journalism. Related paths include editing, anchoring, documentary work, public relations and content strategy.

🧭 The path after Class 10

Stream is not a barrier — any stream suits journalism. Humanities students often feel at home with the writing and current-affairs focus, but science and commerce backgrounds are real assets for science, health, business and data journalism. Read widely and start writing early.

🎓 The path after Class 12

  • BJMC — Bachelor of Journalism & Mass Communication (3 years) — the most direct route; many central universities admit via CUET.
  • BA in Journalism / Mass Communication / English — a broad base, often followed by a specialised Master’s or an MA from IIMC.
  • Diplomas and PG courses in journalism — focused, practical programmes, including broadcast and digital tracks.
  • MA / PG at a top institute — IIMC, Jamia MCRC, Symbiosis (SIMC) and XIC are highly respected for the postgraduate stage.

🛠️ Core skills to build

  • Writing and clarity — turning a complex situation into a story people understand.
  • Curiosity and nerve — asking the question others won’t, and chasing the facts.
  • Verification and ethics — getting it right matters more than getting it first.
  • Digital fluency — video, social, audio and data are now part of the job.

🧩 Is this career right for you?

Journalism suits Artistic interests (writing and storytelling) combined with Investigative (digging for the truth) and Social (engaging with people and the world). If you are curious, articulate and care about what’s actually true, it is a strong fit — and the digital shift has created new routes for people willing to build an audience.

Key takeaways
  • Any stream works; a science or commerce base is an asset for specialised reporting.
  • BJMC via CUET, then a top PG (IIMC, Jamia, SIMC), is a well-trodden route.
  • Digital has reshaped the field — video, data and audience-building are now core.
  • Best-fit interests: Artistic + Investigative + Social. You are curious, articulate and truth-driven.
