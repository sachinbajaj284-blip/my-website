What counselling for low mood can help with in Sonipat

You don’t need a diagnosis or a reason that looks big enough from outside. These are the things people bring when low mood has been weighing on them:

🌁
Loss of interest

Things you used to enjoy going flat — hobbies, people, plans. Often the earliest and most honest sign.

😶
Exhaustion & sleep

Tiredness sleep doesn’t fix, mornings getting harder, everything taking more effort than it should.

🧠
The harsh inner voice

Guilt, feeling like a burden, a running self-criticism you’d never accept aimed at anyone else.

😓
Withdrawing

Pulling back from friends and family without quite deciding to, and the loneliness that follows.

🌑
Numbness, not just sadness

Feeling flat or empty rather than tearful — the version of low mood that’s easiest to miss and dismiss.

🫀
Feeling stuck

Drift after a setback, a loss or a change — where low mood and feeling directionless feed each other.

What the pressure looks like here

Sonipat has changed fast. The campuses out towards Rai — Ashoka, the Jindal global university, the sports school — pull in students from across India and beyond into intense, high-achieving environments, most of them living away from home for the first time. The pressure there is comparison inside an exceptional peer group: very capable people quietly convinced they’re falling behind, on evidence that wouldn’t survive five minutes of scrutiny.

A few kilometres away is the other Sonipat — a Haryana town and an industrial belt around Kundli on the edge of Delhi NCR, with local students and families whose world is very different from the campuses next door. Both carry real strain; they just don’t look alike.

What they share is distance and newness. A first-year a long way from home, a student on a course that turned out wrong, a young person reading an unfamiliar room in an unfamiliar language — and the specific loneliness of being surrounded by people and still feeling that nobody really knows you.

Frequently asked questions

Will my university or its counselling cell be told?

No. This is entirely outside your institution — no note to a cell, no attendance record, nothing to a warden or your parents. You can book using a first name only, which for a lot of students on these campuses is exactly why they use it.

Everyone around me seems to be doing better. Is that worth a session?

Yes, and it’s one of the most common things students here bring. Comparison inside a high-achieving peer group is a genuine and specific difficulty, and it responds well to being examined out loud with someone who isn’t in that peer group.

Is this available in Hindi?

Yes — Hindi or English, whichever you think in. A lot of students study in English and think in Hindi, and there’s no reason a session should run in the harder language.

I have backlogs or a course I regret, and my family doesn’t know. Can I talk about that?

Yes, and it’s very common. The academic problem and the growing gap with home are two different problems, and it usually helps to separate them before deciding anything.

Can counselling help with depression?

Often, yes — especially for mild to moderate low mood, where counselling, support and small changes make a real difference. More severe depression sometimes needs medication alongside, and a counsellor will tell you honestly.

I feel numb rather than sad. Is that still worth talking about?

Yes. Loss of feeling — not enjoying anything, feeling flat or empty — is a core feature of depression and is often missed. It’s very much worth talking through.
