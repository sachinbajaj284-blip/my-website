What your score means

Seven items, each scored 0 (not at all) to 3 (nearly every day), for 21 in total. The usual bands:

  • 0–4 — minimal. Anxiety isn't currently a significant load. Worth re-checking if things change.
  • 5–9 — mild. Noticeable but generally manageable. Often responds well to sleep, routine and having someone to talk to.
  • 10–14 — moderate. Anxiety is likely costing you something — sleep, concentration, enjoyment. A good point to get support rather than push through.
  • 15–21 — severe. A heavy load to be carrying alone. Professional support is genuinely worth arranging.
A more useful question than the number: is worry affecting your sleep, your studies or work, your relationships, or the decisions you're making? If the answer is yes, that's worth talking about regardless of what you scored. Plenty of people with a "mild" score are struggling more than the total suggests.

Why the two-week window matters

The GAD-7 asks about the last fortnight, not your life in general, and that’s on purpose. Anxiety moves. A score taken the week before boards, or the week after a breakup, is measuring that week. Which is the whole idea. It’s a snapshot of load, not a description of your personality, and it isn’t a label you’re now stuck with.

Which also means one high score isn’t a catastrophe, and one low score isn’t proof you’re fine. If you’re unsure, take it again in a fortnight and see whether the picture holds.

What actually helps with anxiety

None of this replaces support, but all of it is worth doing while you decide:

  • Breathe out longer than you breathe in. A four-count in, six-count out, for two minutes. It's the fastest reliable way to bring a spiking body down, and it works because the long exhale is what your nervous system reads as safety.
  • Protect sleep before anything else. Anxiety and sleep debt amplify each other viciously. Fixing sleep often lowers a GAD-7 score more than anything you consciously do about the worry itself.
  • Put the catastrophe on paper. Write down what you're actually afraid will happen, in full. Worry loses a surprising amount of force once it has to hold still and be specific.
  • Move daily, badly is fine. A walk counts. Consistency matters far more than intensity here.
  • Say it out loud to one person. The single biggest predictor of anxiety getting worse is carrying it silently.

When self-help isn't enough

Techniques work well while you can still get some distance from the anxiety. They work less well once worry has become the background hum of your day, once you’re avoiding things you used to handle, or once the physical side — the chest, the stomach, the sleep — has stopped responding to anything you try.

That’s the stage where talking to someone qualified stops being an overreaction and starts being the quicker route. Not because you’re broken. Because anxiety is very hard to see straight from the inside.

Frequently asked questions

Is this anxiety test free?

Completely. No sign-up, no email, no payment. Your answers aren't stored, sent or seen by anyone — they stay in your browser for the length of the check and are gone when you close the tab.

Does this test diagnose anxiety?

No. The GAD-7 is a screening and reflection scale, not a diagnostic instrument. It tells you roughly how much anxiety you've been carrying — useful information — but only a qualified clinician can diagnose an anxiety disorder, and that takes a proper assessment.

What is the GAD-7?

The Generalised Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale: a short, widely used questionnaire asking how often seven common anxiety symptoms have bothered you over two weeks. Each answer scores 0–3, giving a total out of 21.

Is it anonymous?

Yes. No name, email or phone number to take it, and nothing recorded. If you later book a session, a first name is enough.

I got a high score. What now?

A high score is information, not a verdict — anxiety responds well to support. A sensible next step is one confidential conversation to work out what's driving it. If you're in immediate distress or having thoughts of self-harm, call Tele-MANAS on 14416 rather than waiting for a session.
