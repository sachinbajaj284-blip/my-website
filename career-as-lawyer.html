How to Become a Lawyer in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Law is one of the most versatile careers in India — a single degree opens litigation, corporate practice, the judiciary, civil services, policy and more. It is a stream-open, reasoning-driven path that rewards strong reading, argument and diligence over any particular subject background.

What a lawyer actually does

Lawyers advise, argue and protect rights — but the daily work depends heavily on the branch. A litigator appears in court and drafts pleadings; a corporate lawyer negotiates contracts, mergers and compliance for companies; an in-house counsel guides a single organisation; and law graduates also enter the judiciary (through judicial services exams), civil services, policy and academia. The common core: careful reading, sharp reasoning and precise writing.

The path after Class 10

Any stream can lead to law, so choose on your wider interests. Humanities is a natural home — the reading, history and political-science exposure help — but Commerce and Science students become excellent lawyers too. What genuinely matters is strong English, reading comprehension and logical reasoning, all of which the entrance exams test.

The path after Class 12

  • CLAT — the Common Law Admission Test for the 5-year integrated BA LLB at most National Law Universities (NLUs).
  • AILET — the separate entrance for NLU Delhi (National Law University, Delhi).
  • Other routes — LSAT–India (Jindal and others), state law entrances, and university-level exams.
  • 3-year LLB — taken after any bachelor’s degree, an alternative to the 5-year route.
  • Enrolment — after the degree, register with a State Bar Council and clear the All India Bar Examination (AIBE) to practise.

Is this career right for you?

Law suits students with strong Enterprising and Social interests in the Holland RIASEC model — persuasion, advocacy and working with people — usually paired with Investigative curiosity for research and argument. It rewards patience: the early litigation years can be slow. If you enjoy reading closely, arguing well and standing up for a position, this is a strong fit.

Key takeaways
  • Any stream works; strong English and reasoning matter more than subjects.
  • The main route is CLAT/AILET → 5-year BA LLB → Bar enrolment (AIBE).
  • Pay ranges widely — top NLU + corporate firm is very different from early litigation.
  • Best-fit interests: Enterprising + Social + Investigative, with patience and diligence.
