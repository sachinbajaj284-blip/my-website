What your score means

Seventeen items scored out of 68. The usual bands:

  • 0–18 — below the threshold. Social nerves within the normal range. Most people feel some of this.
  • 19–30 — mild. Noticeable social anxiety. Often responds well to gradually facing what you avoid.
  • 31–40 — moderate. Likely shaping your choices — which events you skip, what you won't do. A good point to get support.
  • 41–50 — severe. A heavy load that's probably narrowing your life. Professional support is genuinely worth arranging.
  • 51–68 — very severe. Social fear is dominating. Talking to someone qualified is the quicker route, not an overreaction.
A more useful question than the number: what have you stopped doing because of the fear? Avoidance is the clearest sign social anxiety is costing you something — and the thing therapy works on first.

What actually helps with social anxiety

None of this replaces support, but all of it is worth doing while you decide:

  • Approach, don't avoid. Every time you dodge a feared situation, the fear gets stronger. Facing it in small, repeated steps is what shrinks it — this is the heart of the treatment.
  • Drop the safety behaviours. Rehearsing every sentence, gripping your glass, avoiding eye contact — these feel protective but keep the fear in place.
  • Shift attention outward. Social anxiety traps you in self-monitoring. Deliberately focusing on the other person, not on how you're coming across, breaks the loop.
  • Test the prediction. Ask what you fear will happen, then check what actually did. The gap is usually large and informative.
  • Start where it's survivable. Build a ladder from mildly uncomfortable to terrifying, and climb it a rung at a time rather than leaping.

When self-help isn't enough

Facing fears works well while you can still get some distance from the anxiety. It works less well once avoidance has quietly reorganised your life — the opportunities skipped, the friendships not made, the roles not applied for. That's the stage where a therapist's structure matters: social anxiety responds specifically and strongly to CBT with graded exposure, which is much easier to do with someone guiding the steps than alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is this test free and private?

Completely. No sign-up, no email, no payment. Your answers aren't stored, sent or seen by anyone — they stay in your browser for the length of the check and are gone when you close the tab.

Does this diagnose social anxiety disorder?

No. The SPIN is a screening and severity scale, not a diagnostic instrument. It tells you how much social anxiety you're carrying — only a qualified clinician can diagnose a disorder.

Is this just shyness?

Shyness is a temperament; social anxiety is when fear of judgement starts limiting what you do. The clue is avoidance — skipping things you'd otherwise want to do because of the fear.

What is the SPIN?

The Social Phobia Inventory is a seventeen-item questionnaire covering fear, avoidance and physical symptoms, each scored 0–4, for a total out of 68. Around 19 or above often indicates social anxiety.

Is it anonymous?

Yes. No name, email or phone number to take it, and nothing recorded. If you later book a session, a first name is enough.
