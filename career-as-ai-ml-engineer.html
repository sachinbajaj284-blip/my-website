How to Become an AI/ML Engineer in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

An AI/ML engineer builds the systems that learn — the models behind recommendations, chatbots, vision and the current wave of generative AI. It is among the highest-paid and fastest-moving tech careers, and it is genuinely maths-heavy; the hype is real, and so is the rigour.

What an AI/ML engineer actually does

An AI/ML engineer designs, trains and deploys machine-learning models, and builds the pipelines that run them in production. It sits between research and software engineering: more building and deployment than a data scientist, more modelling than a general software engineer. Specialisations include computer vision, natural language processing, generative AI and MLOps. The field is close to the research frontier, so continuous learning is part of the job, not an extra.

The path after Class 10

Take Science with Mathematics (PCM). This field runs on linear algebra, probability and calculus, so a strong maths base is the single most important thing you can build early — more important than any specific tool or language.

The path after Class 12

  • B.Tech in CSE / AI / Data Science (4 years) — the most common route, via JEE Main/Advanced or state/private entrances.
  • B.Sc / BS in Mathematics, Statistics or Computer Science → Master’s — a strong analytical base, often followed by an M.Tech/M.Sc in AI or ML.
  • BCA / B.Sc CS → specialised ML programme — a coding-first route, backed by serious self-study in the maths.
  • Self-taught + portfolio — real projects, open-source contributions and a public portfolio carry genuine weight here, alongside a degree.

Core skills to build

  • Mathematics — linear algebra, probability and calculus — the real foundation, not optional.
  • Programming — Python first, with strong software-engineering fundamentals.
  • ML & deep learning — how models work, and PyTorch/TensorFlow to build them.
  • Deployment (MLOps) — getting a model out of a notebook and into reliable production.

Is this career right for you?

AI/ML engineering suits strong Investigative interests (a genuine pull toward maths and problem-solving) combined with Realistic (building working systems). If you enjoy the maths rather than tolerating it, and you like building things that work rather than only theorising, it is an excellent — and currently very well-paid — fit.

Key takeaways
  • Maths is the foundation — PCM, then a CS/AI or maths-heavy route; the hype rides on real rigour.
  • A B.Tech is the common path, but projects and a portfolio carry serious weight too.
  • It is close to the research frontier, so continuous learning is part of the job.
  • Best-fit interests: Investigative + Realistic. You enjoy the maths and like building systems that work.
