How to Become a Commercial Pilot in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated July 2026 · 8 min read

Flying an aircraft is one of the most exciting careers there is — and one of the most demanding on discipline, health and finances. The path is skill- and licence-based rather than exam-rank based, but it is a serious investment that deserves a clear-eyed decision.

What a commercial pilot actually does

Pilots operate aircraft safely from departure to arrival — but the job is far more than the moments in the air. It is meticulous pre-flight planning, weather and route decisions, checklists, coordination with air traffic control and crew, and continuous responsibility for the safety of everyone on board. It demands calm under pressure, sharp situational awareness and lifelong discipline about health and recurrency checks.

The path after Class 10

You must take Science with Physics and Mathematics (PCM) in Class 11–12 — these are mandatory for a Commercial Pilot Licence. If you have already chosen another stream, you can add Physics and Maths later through NIOS to become eligible, so a wrong early choice is recoverable here.

The path after Class 12

  1. Join a DGCA-approved flying school — in India or abroad.
  2. Clear DGCA ground exams — air navigation, meteorology, air regulations, technical subjects.
  3. Pass the DGCA Class 1 medical — strict fitness and eyesight standards apply; check eligibility early.
  4. Log the required flying hours — and earn your Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL).
  5. Type-rating — training on a specific aircraft (e.g. A320/B737) before an airline job.
Check the DGCA Class 1 medical requirements before you spend anything. Certain vision or health conditions can disqualify a candidate, and it is far better to know at the start than after investing lakhs.

Cost & time

A CPL in India typically costs around ₹35–60 lakh depending on the flying school, hours flown and type-rating, and takes roughly 18 months to 3 years to complete. This is one of the few careers where the financial plan is as important as the aptitude — families should budget honestly before committing.

Is this career right for you?

Piloting suits Realistic interests in the Holland RIASEC model (hands-on operation of complex machines) paired with Investigative and Conventional strengths (systematic thinking, strict adherence to procedure). Temperament is decisive: airlines and the job itself reward people who stay calm, follow checklists precisely, and take safety seriously every single time. If precision and responsibility energise you rather than tire you, this is a strong fit.

Key takeaways
  • PCM is required; missing subjects can be added via NIOS.
  • The route is licence-based: flying school → DGCA exams → medical → CPL → type-rating.
  • Training costs ₹35–60 lakh — plan finances and check the Class 1 medical first.
  • Best-fit interests: Realistic + Investigative + Conventional, with rock-steady discipline.
