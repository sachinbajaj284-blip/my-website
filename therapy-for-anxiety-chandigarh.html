What anxiety counselling can help with in Chandigarh

You don’t need a diagnosis to book, and you don’t need to be in crisis. These are the forms anxiety most often takes when people reach out:

🧠
Worry that won’t stop

A mind that jumps from one “what if” to the next and never quite settles, even when nothing’s wrong.

💓
The physical side

Racing heart, tight chest, a knotted stomach, restlessness, muscles that stay tense even at rest.

💨
Panic attacks

Sudden surges of fear with pounding heart and breathlessness — frightening, and very workable with support.

😴
Sleep & focus

Lying awake with a racing mind, trouble concentrating, feeling on edge and exhausted at once.

🚫
Avoidance

Turning down things you want to do, or getting through them braced — the quiet cost anxiety adds up over time.

📚
Exam & performance anxiety

Blanking despite preparing, dread before presentations, panic that peaks at exactly the wrong moment.

What the pressure looks like here

Chandigarh is small and socially dense in a way the big metros aren’t. Families know each other, neighbourhoods stay put, and the same names turn up across schools, clubs and offices. Mostly that’s pleasant. In one respect it’s a problem: privacy is hard here, and being seen is a real reason people don’t get help.

Two things come with the region. One is a strong habit of appearing fine. Distress is something you handle at home, not something you discuss. The other is migration. An enormous number of tricity families have a child abroad or getting ready to go, and the pressure around IELTS, visas and the money already committed is its own local stressor. So is what happens when the plan falls through, or when the child who went is struggling alone on the other side of the world.

There’s a sizeable student population too, across Panjab University and the Mohali belt, carrying the usual academic pressure and the usual business of becoming your own person.

What this is, and what it isn’t

It’s counselling support, not treatment: confidential conversations about what you’re carrying and what might help. Frequently asked questions

Chandigarh is small and I don’t want to be seen. Is this really private?

Yes, and it is the main reason people here choose an online session. There is no clinic, no waiting room and no building to be seen entering. Nothing is reported to anyone, and you can book using a first name only.

Can we talk in Punjabi?

Sessions run in Hindi or English. If Punjabi is the language you think in, say so at the start — a lot can be accommodated in how a session runs, and it is better to raise it than to work in a language that keeps the difficult things at arm's length.

My child has gone abroad and is struggling. Can we get help from here?

Yes. A parent session is a reasonable place to start, and your child can also book independently — sessions are online, so distance isn’t an obstacle. Struggling alone overseas is common and rarely mentioned home in full.

How is this different from PGIMER?

Different purpose. PGIMER is a major clinical institution and the right place for psychiatric assessment and serious need. This is non-diagnostic counselling support — conversations for people who are struggling but not ill, without a referral, a waiting list, or a corridor.

Can counselling actually help my anxiety?

Yes. Anxiety is highly manageable — counselling helps you understand what keeps it going and build skills to lower it. For many people the change is large.

Is the anxiety check a diagnosis?

No. The GAD-7 is a brief reflection scale that tells you roughly how heavy the last couple of weeks have been. Only a qualified professional can diagnose, and your answers aren’t stored.
