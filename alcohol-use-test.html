What your score means

Ten items scored out of 40. The usual bands:

  • 0–7 — lower risk. Drinking is within generally low-risk limits. Worth re-checking if things change.
  • 8–15 — increasing risk. A pattern worth noticing. Simple changes now are much easier than later.
  • 16–19 — higher risk. Drinking is likely costing you something. A good point to get support rather than push through.
  • 20–40 — possible dependence. Worth a proper assessment, and — if stopping brings on shakes or sweats — medical advice on a safe taper.
A more useful question than the number: have you tried to cut down and found it harder than expected? If yes, that's worth talking about whatever you scored.

What actually helps with drinking

None of this replaces support, but all of it is worth doing while you decide:

  • Count in standard drinks, honestly. A 'couple of drinks' is often four or five units. The real number is usually the thing that changes your mind.
  • Put days between sessions. Alcohol-free days each week do more for your liver, sleep and tolerance than cutting the amount on drinking days.
  • Treat the job it's doing. If drinking is managing stress, sleep or social nerves, deal with that directly and the pull eases.
  • Don't stop cold turkey if you're heavily dependent. Withdrawal from heavy regular drinking can be medically dangerous. Get a doctor's guidance on tapering safely.
  • Tell one person. Secrecy keeps drinking going. One honest conversation changes the odds.

When self-help isn't enough

Techniques work while drinking is still a choice you can steer. They work less well once you've tried to cut down and couldn't, once the morning-after guilt is routine, or once stopping brings on physical withdrawal. That's the stage where talking to someone qualified is the quicker route — and, if you're physically dependent, where a doctor should guide a safe taper rather than willpower alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is this test free and private?

Completely. No sign-up, no email, no payment. Your answers aren't stored, sent or seen by anyone — they stay in your browser for the length of the check and are gone when you close the tab.

Does this diagnose alcohol use disorder?

No. The AUDIT is a screening scale, not a diagnostic tool. It tells you roughly how risky your drinking pattern is — useful information — but only a qualified professional can diagnose a disorder.

What is the AUDIT?

The Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test is a ten-item questionnaire developed by the World Health Organisation. Each item scores 0–4, giving a total out of 40 that falls into low, increasing, higher or dependence-range risk.

Is it anonymous?

Yes. No name, email or phone number to take it, and nothing recorded. If you later book a session, a first name is enough.

I scored high. What should I do?

A high score is a reason to talk to a professional, not a verdict. If you drink heavily every day and stopping brings on shakes, sweating or anxiety, see a doctor before cutting down — withdrawal can be dangerous. In immediate distress, call Tele-MANAS on 14416.
