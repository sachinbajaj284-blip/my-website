How to Become a Physiotherapist in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Physiotherapy is helping people move and recover — after injury, surgery, stroke or in chronic pain. It is hands-on, growing fast with India’s interest in sports and fitness, and it rewards patience as much as skill.

What a physiotherapist actually does

A physiotherapist assesses movement and physical function, then uses exercise, manual therapy, and techniques like electrotherapy to restore it. They work in hospitals, rehab centres, sports teams, orthopaedic and neuro clinics, and increasingly in private practice and home care. Specialisations (via MPT) include sports, orthopaedics, neurology, paediatrics and cardiorespiratory physiotherapy.

The path after Class 10

Take Science with Biology (PCB). Biology underpins the anatomy and physiology that physiotherapy is built on; some universities admit PCM students too, but PCB is the cleaner path.

The path after Class 12

  • BPT — Bachelor of Physiotherapy (4.5 years, incl. a 6-month internship) — the core degree; admission by university or state entrance, or merit, depending on the institution (NEET is not usually required).
  • MPT — Master of Physiotherapy (2 years) — specialisation in sports, ortho, neuro, paediatrics or cardiorespiratory; strongly recommended for growth.
  • Practice, sports or academia — clinics, hospitals, sports teams, your own practice, or teaching and research.

Core skills to build

  • Anatomy and physiology — a deep, working understanding of how the body moves.
  • Hands-on technique — manual therapy and exercise prescription done skilfully.
  • Patience and motivation — recovery is slow; you keep people going through it.
  • Communication — explaining and coaching exercises people will do on their own.

Is this career right for you?

Physiotherapy suits Social interests (a drive to help people directly) combined with Realistic (hands-on, physical work) and some Investigative (assessment and problem-solving). If you are patient, physically engaged and genuinely satisfied by someone’s slow return to movement, it fits well.

Key takeaways
  • BPT is the route; NEET is usually not required, unlike MBBS or BDS.
  • An MPT specialisation (especially sports or neuro) drives growth and pay.
  • Demand is rising with India’s sports, fitness and ageing-care needs.
  • Best-fit interests: Social + Realistic + Investigative. You like hands-on work that helps people recover.
