What the pressure looks like here

Gurugram is an unusual city psychologically: a very large share of the people in it moved here for work in the last decade, which means the ordinary support structure most people rely on — family nearby, friends from school, a neighbourhood that knows you — is simply absent. The people around you are colleagues, and colleagues are also the people you are being measured against.

The result is a distinct pattern of workplace strain: high demands and reasonable pay, but low control, thin support and almost no recovery. Add a commute that eats two hours and a rental cost that makes leaving a job feel risky, and you get people who are not in crisis but have been running at a level they could not sustain for years.

The other Gurugram pattern is the parent working these hours who is worried about a teenager they barely see on weekdays, and who cannot tell whether what they are noticing is adolescence or something more.

What this is — and what it is not

This is non-diagnostic counselling support: structured, confidential conversations about what you are dealing with and what would practically help. This is not a diagnosis, a prescription, or an emergency service. We do not label conditions and we do not prescribe medication. If what you describe would be better served by a psychiatrist, your counsellor will say so in the first session rather than sell you a package.

Frequently asked questions

Will my employer know I booked a session?

No. This is completely independent of any corporate wellness programme — your company is not involved, is not billed, and is not told. You can book using a first name only.

Can I do a session outside office hours?

Yes. You pick your own slot from the live calendar, and evening and weekend times are available. Sessions are online, so there is no commute added to your day.

My problem is genuinely my workload. Can counselling fix that?

It cannot change your workload, and it would be dishonest to claim otherwise. What it can help with is the part you do control — boundaries, what you want to ask for, how you are reading the situation, and whether staying is the right call. Sometimes the honest conclusion is that the job is the problem.
