What your score means

Each yes scores one point, out of five.

  • 0–2 — below the threshold. Below the usual screening cut-off. If something still feels unresolved, it's worth talking about regardless.
  • 3–5 — at or above the threshold. A positive screen. This is a reason to talk to a professional about a fuller assessment — not a diagnosis, and not something to carry alone.
A screen is a signpost, not a verdict. Three or more yes answers is the usual cut-off for a positive screen — but if even one of these is making daily life hard, that's reason enough to reach out.

What actually helps after trauma

None of this replaces support, but all of it is worth doing while you decide:

  • Don't force the memory, don't bury it either. Avoiding all reminders keeps trauma frozen; forcing yourself to relive it can re-traumatise. Gentle, supported processing is the middle path — which is what trauma therapy provides.
  • Anchor in the present. When a memory intrudes, name five things you can see and feel right now. Grounding reminds your body the danger is over.
  • Protect sleep and routine. Trauma scrambles both. Regular meals, movement and a steady sleep window give your nervous system a floor to stand on.
  • Keep connection, even small. Numbing and withdrawal are classic trauma responses and they make it worse. One safe person you stay in contact with matters.
  • Go easy on alcohol and substances. They numb short-term and deepen trauma symptoms long-term. If you're leaning on them, that's worth mentioning to someone.

When self-help isn't enough

Self-help steadies the ground, but trauma often needs more than steadying. If the nightmares, flashbacks, hypervigilance or numbness have lasted beyond a month and are shaping your days, that's the stage for proper support. PTSD responds well to specific trauma-focused therapies — and these are much safer done with a trained professional than attempted alone. Reaching out isn't weakness after what you've been through; it's the thing that lets it heal.

Frequently asked questions

Is this test free and private?

Completely. No sign-up, no email, no payment. Your answers aren't stored, sent or seen by anyone — they stay in your browser for the length of the check and are gone when you close the tab.

Does this diagnose PTSD?

No. The PC-PTSD-5 is a brief screen, not a diagnostic tool. A positive result means a fuller assessment is worth pursuing — only a qualified clinician can diagnose PTSD.

What counts as 'trauma'?

Any event that was frightening, dangerous or overwhelming — an accident, assault, abuse, disaster, sudden loss, a frightening medical event, or witnessing something terrible. Trauma is about impact, not how it compares to others'.

What is the PC-PTSD-5?

The Primary Care PTSD Screen for DSM-5 is a five-item yes/no screen used widely in healthcare settings. Three or more yes answers is the usual threshold for a positive screen.

Is it anonymous?

Yes. No name, email or phone number to take it, and nothing recorded. If you later book a session, a first name is enough.
