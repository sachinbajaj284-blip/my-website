How to Become a Company Secretary in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

A Company Secretary is a company’s governance and compliance expert — the person who keeps it on the right side of corporate law. It is a professional qualification (like CA), run by the ICSI, and open to students from any stream.

What a company secretary actually does

A Company Secretary ensures a company complies with the Companies Act, SEBI regulations and other corporate law; manages board and shareholder meetings and their records; advises directors on governance; and handles filings with regulators. In listed companies the CS is a Key Managerial Personnel by law. The role blends law, finance and administration, and leads naturally into company-secretarial, legal and compliance leadership.

The path after Class 10

Stream matters less here than for most careers. Commerce is the natural fit and makes the finance and law papers easier, but students from any stream can become a CS. What matters is comfort with reading, law and detail.

The path after Class 12

  • CSEET — CS Executive Entrance Test — the entry test after Class 12 (graduates may get exemptions).
  • CS Executive — the first main level, covering company law, tax, accounting and more.
  • CS Professional — the final level, with advanced governance, law and strategy papers.
  • Practical training — a period of mandatory training with a company or practising CS, alongside the Professional stage, before membership of the ICSI.

Core skills to build

  • Knowledge of corporate law — the Companies Act, SEBI rules and allied regulation.
  • Precision and diligence — compliance leaves no room for sloppy detail.
  • Communication and drafting — minutes, resolutions, reports and board advice.
  • Ethics and judgement — you are a guardian of governance, not just a filer.

Is this career right for you?

The CS path suits Conventional interests (structure, rules, precision) combined with Enterprising (advising leadership and navigating business). If you like law and finance but prefer governance and compliance to courtroom litigation or pure accounting, it is a focused, respected fit.

Key takeaways
  • It is a professional qualification via the ICSI — CSEET, then Executive, then Professional, plus training.
  • Any stream can enter, though Commerce makes the law and finance papers easier.
  • In listed companies the CS is Key Managerial Personnel — a senior, well-paid governance role.
  • Best-fit interests: Conventional + Enterprising. You like law and business with structure and responsibility.
