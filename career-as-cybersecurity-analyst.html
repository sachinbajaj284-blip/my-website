How to Become a Cybersecurity Analyst in India

By the Lume Live counselling team · Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

A cybersecurity analyst defends systems and data from attack — finding weaknesses before attackers do, and responding when they strike. As everything moves online, demand has outstripped supply, which makes it one of the most certification-driven and opportunity-rich tech careers.

What a cybersecurity analyst actually does

A cybersecurity analyst monitors networks and systems for threats, investigates incidents, hardens defences, and tests for vulnerabilities. The field branches widely: a Security Operations Centre (SOC) analyst watches for and responds to attacks; a penetration tester (ethical hacker) deliberately breaks in to find holes; others work in governance, risk and compliance, application security or digital forensics. Certifications matter unusually much here, often as much as the degree.

The path after Class 10

Take Science with Mathematics (PCM) for the strongest base into a computing degree. Logical thinking and comfort with computers matter most; a curiosity about how systems work — and break — is the real early signal.

The path after Class 12

  • B.Tech in CSE / Cybersecurity (4 years) — via JEE or state/private entrances; a broad, respected base.
  • BCA / B.Sc Computer Science — solid routes, especially when paired with security certifications.
  • Certifications — CompTIA Security+, CEH (ethical hacking), and later OSCP or CISSP — these carry real hiring weight in this field.
  • Hands-on practice — labs, capture-the-flag (CTF) competitions and a home lab build the practical skill employers test for.

Core skills to build

  • Networking & systems — how networks, operating systems and the web actually work.
  • Security fundamentals — threats, cryptography, access control and defence-in-depth.
  • Scripting — Python and shell scripting to automate and investigate.
  • Analytical, ethical mindset — thinking like an attacker while acting responsibly.

Is this career right for you?

Cybersecurity suits Investigative interests (curiosity about how things work and break) with Conventional (methodical rigour) and a Realistic hands-on streak. If you like puzzles, think like someone probing for the weak point, and have a strong ethical compass, it is a genuinely good fit — and one where skill and certifications can outrun the college name.

Key takeaways
  • PCM into a computing degree is the base; BCA and B.Sc CS also work well.
  • Certifications (Security+, CEH, OSCP) carry unusual weight — sometimes as much as the degree.
  • Hands-on practice (labs, CTFs, bug bounties) is how you actually get hired.
  • Best-fit interests: Investigative + Conventional + Realistic. You like puzzles and probing how things break — ethically.
