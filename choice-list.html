Get your choice list in the right order

JoSAA gives you the highest choice on your list that your rank qualifies for. That one rule decides everything — and it means most students order their list in a way that quietly costs them a better seat. Build your list here and this will tell you where it goes wrong.

        What students get wrong

        Does putting an ambitious choice first reduce my chances?

        No. If you do not qualify for it, the system simply moves to your next choice. There is no penalty, no "wasted" choice, and no signal sent to the institute. This is the single most common misunderstanding in JoSAA counselling.

        Then what actually goes wrong?

        Putting safe options near the top. Allotment stops at the first choice you qualify for — so a safe choice at number three means choices four onwards can never reach you, however much you would have preferred them.

        How many choices should I fill?

        More than you think. There is no limit and no cost to a long list. The real risk runs the other way: a short list of ambitious choices with no safe option at the bottom can leave you with no allotment at all.

        Should I include a branch I do not want?

        Only below everything you do want, and only if you would genuinely accept it. A choice list is a statement of what you will take — if a seat is allotted and you accept it, that closes your other options for the round.

        Is this list official?

        No. This is a planning aid built on published JoSAA closing ranks. You still have to enter and lock your actual choices on the JoSAA portal, and only what you submit there counts.
