Talking to someone qualified in Kolkata — without the awkwardness

What the pressure looks like here

Kolkata takes education seriously in a way few cities do, and that is a genuine strength — but it has a flip side. When marks, a university place and a respectable profession are treated as the measure of a family’s effort, a student who is struggling reads their own difficulty as a kind of betrayal. The pressure is rarely loud. It sits under the surface, in a household that values achievement and doesn’t quite have the vocabulary for distress.

There is a second, very Kolkata strain too: the young person deciding whether to leave. Many of the best opportunities are in other cities, and the choice to go — or to stay and feel left behind — carries guilt, about ageing parents, about a city they love, about expectations they didn’t set. It is a heavy thing to carry at twenty-two, and most carry it alone.

What is already available in Kolkata, and what’s missing

Kolkata has real psychiatric depth. The Institute of Psychiatry, attached to the SSKM/IPGMER campus, is one of the oldest psychiatry teaching institutions in the country, and the state hospital at Gobra provides public care. If you need a psychiatric assessment, or things are severe, those are the right places to go.

What is much harder to find is an unhurried, private conversation for someone who isn’t ill but is struggling — the student who can’t concentrate, the graduate agonising over whether to leave, the person who has gone flat and can’t explain it. Public psychiatry has waiting lists and a clinical threshold to cross first; private therapy in the city runs ₹1,500 to ₹3,000 a session, which for a student often means not going.

Sessions here happen online, nothing reaches your family or anyone else, and a session is ₹499.

What this is, and what it isn’t

It’s counselling support, not treatment: confidential conversations about what you’re carrying and what might help. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to travel anywhere in Kolkata for a session?

No. Sessions are online by video, voice or chat, so there’s no clinic to reach and no waiting room to sit in. For many people in Kolkata, not being seen walking into one is exactly the point.

Can I speak in Bengali?

Sessions run in Hindi and English, and you’re welcome to slip in the Bengali words that carry the feeling — most people don’t describe their inner life in textbook language anyway. What matters is that you can say what’s going on without first translating it.

I’m torn about leaving the city for a job and feel guilty. Is that worth a session?

Yes — it’s exactly the kind of thing worth talking through. The decision to leave or stay is rarely just practical; it carries guilt about family and place that’s hard to weigh alone. A single conversation with someone outside it often brings real clarity.

Will my family find out?

No. Nothing is reported to your family, and you can book using a first name only. The one limit, which every counsellor works under, is where there is serious risk to life — and even then it’s a conversation with you, not behind your back.
