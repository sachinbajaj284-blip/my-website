What counselling for sleep problems can help with

You don’t need a diagnosis to reach out. These are the things people bring when sleep has become a struggle:

🧠
The racing mind at night

Lying awake as the day replays and tomorrow’s worries arrive right on cue, just as you need to switch off.

⏰
The 3am wake-up

Falling asleep fine, then waking in the small hours and being unable to drop off again.

😟
Dread of bedtime

Anxiety about whether you’ll sleep that builds through the evening — and makes sleep even less likely.

🔋
The daytime toll

Exhaustion, low mood, irritability and foggy focus that follow a run of bad nights.

📱
Habits that backfire

Scrolling in bed, clock-watching, napping, caffeine — the coping that quietly keeps insomnia going.

🔄
Worry and sleep feeding each other

Stress wrecks sleep, and poor sleep worsens stress — a loop worth breaking from both ends.

Frequently asked questions

Can counselling really help me sleep?

Yes — for most people, persistent insomnia is kept going by the anxiety and habits that build around sleep, and those are very workable. The best-evidenced approach, CBT for insomnia (CBT-I), targets exactly that, and counselling support can help you put its principles into practice.

Should I just take sleeping pills?

Sleep medication can have a short-term place, but it’s a doctor’s decision and it doesn’t address what keeps insomnia going. For long-term change, working on the worry and habits around sleep tends to help more and lasts. If there may be an underlying medical cause, it’s worth getting that checked too.

Is the self-check a diagnosis?

No. It’s a brief reflection tool, not a diagnosis, and your answers aren’t stored. Persistent sleep problems can have medical causes, so if that looks likely, your counsellor will suggest getting it assessed.

Will anyone find out?

No. Nothing is shared with your parents, school, college or employer — no report, no summary, not even confirmation that you attended. A first name is enough to book.
