What counselling for low mood can help with in Bangalore

You don’t need a diagnosis or a reason that looks big enough from outside. These are the things people bring when low mood has been weighing on them:

🌁
Loss of interest

Things you used to enjoy going flat — hobbies, people, plans. Often the earliest and most honest sign.

😶
Exhaustion & sleep

Tiredness sleep doesn’t fix, mornings getting harder, everything taking more effort than it should.

🧠
The harsh inner voice

Guilt, feeling like a burden, a running self-criticism you’d never accept aimed at anyone else.

😓
Withdrawing

Pulling back from friends and family without quite deciding to, and the loneliness that follows.

🌑
Numbness, not just sadness

Feeling flat or empty rather than tearful — the version of low mood that’s easiest to miss and dismiss.

🫀
Feeling stuck

Drift after a setback, a loss or a change — where low mood and feeling directionless feed each other.

What the pressure looks like here

The pressure here is comparison, inside a peer group that happens to be exceptional. When everyone around you is an engineer, a founder, or someone whose company just closed a round, a perfectly good career starts feeling like falling behind. Very capable people sit down for a first session convinced they’re failing, on evidence that wouldn’t survive five minutes of scrutiny.

Startups sharpen it. Equity that might be worth nothing, runway measured in months, and a culture where total absorption is the floor rather than the ceiling. High stakes, little control, and no point at which anyone tells you it’s fine to stop. Layoff cycles added a background hum of insecurity that people keep to themselves, because saying it out loud feels like conceding something.

Under the tech story sits a large student population. Engineering, design and management colleges pulling people in from across the south and well beyond, with the same first-year isolation any move produces.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from going to NIMHANS?

Different purpose. NIMHANS is a major clinical institution and the right place for psychiatric assessment, diagnosis and severe or complex need. This is non-diagnostic counselling support — unhurried conversations for people who are struggling but not ill. If what you describe needs psychiatric care, we will say so.

I have a demanding job. Are there slots outside work hours?

Yes. You pick your own slot, and evening and weekend times are available. Sessions are online, so no commute is added to a day that probably has enough in it.

Everyone around me seems to be doing better. Is that worth a session?

Yes, and it is one of the most common things people bring here. Comparison inside a high-achieving peer group is a genuine and specific difficulty, not a trivial one, and it responds well to being examined out loud with someone who isn’t in that peer group.

Will my employer or my team know?

No. This is independent of any corporate wellness programme — your employer isn’t involved, not billed and not told, and a first name is enough to book.

Can counselling help with depression?

Often, yes — especially for mild to moderate low mood, where counselling, support and small changes make a real difference. More severe depression sometimes needs medication alongside, and a counsellor will tell you honestly.

I feel numb rather than sad. Is that still worth talking about?

Yes. Loss of feeling — not enjoying anything, feeling flat or empty — is a core feature of depression and is often missed. It’s very much worth talking through.
