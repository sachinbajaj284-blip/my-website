Talking to someone qualified in Chandigarh — without the awkwardness

What the pressure looks like here

Chandigarh is small, well-connected and socially dense in a way the big metros are not. Families know each other, neighbourhoods are stable, and the same names come up across schools, clubs and offices. That is pleasant in most respects and a genuine obstacle in one: privacy is hard, and the risk of being seen is a real reason people do not seek help here.

The regional context adds two things. First, a strong culture of appearing to be doing well — distress is something you manage at home rather than discuss. Second, the long shadow of migration: an enormous number of families in the tricity have a child abroad or preparing to go, and the pressure around IELTS, visas and the money committed to that route is a distinctive local stressor. So is what happens when that plan fails, or when the child who went is struggling alone overseas.

There is also a substantial student population across Panjab University and the Mohali belt, with the ordinary academic and first-independence pressures.

What is already available in Chandigarh, and what is missing

Chandigarh has PGIMER, whose department of psychiatry is among the strongest in the country. For diagnosis and serious clinical need, the tricity is better served than most of India.

But being well served clinically does not solve the visibility problem, and in a city this size that problem is decisive. People describe not going precisely because of who might see them in the corridor.

Sessions here are online, which removes that entirely — there is no building to be seen entering. A first session is ₹249.

Confidential means confidential

The most common reason people in Chandigarh do not book is not the price. It is the fear that someone will find out. So, plainly:

  • Nothing is shared with your parents, school, college or employer. No report, no summary, no confirmation that you attended.
  • You can book using a first name only. You do not owe us your full name or your reason in advance.
  • The self-checks store nothing. Your answers stay in your browser and are gone when you close the tab.
  • Sessions are online, so there is no waiting room and nobody to run into.
There is one limit, the same one every counsellor works under: if there is a serious risk to your life or someone else's, your counsellor will talk with you about involving someone who can keep you safe. It will be discussed with you, not around you.

What this is — and what it is not

This is non-diagnostic counselling support: structured, confidential conversations about what you are dealing with and what would practically help. This is not a diagnosis, a prescription, or an emergency service. We do not label conditions and we do not prescribe medication. If what you describe would be better served by a psychiatrist, your counsellor will say so in the first session rather than sell you a package.

Frequently asked questions

Chandigarh is small and I do not want to be seen. Is this really private?

Yes, and it is the main reason people here choose an online session. There is no clinic, no waiting room and no building to be seen entering. Nothing is reported to anyone, and you can book using a first name only.

Can we talk in Punjabi?

Sessions run in Hindi or English. If Punjabi is the language you think in, say so at the start — a lot can be accommodated in how a session runs, and it is better to raise it than to work in a language that keeps the difficult things at arm's length.

My child has gone abroad and is struggling. Can we get help from here?

Yes. A parent session is a reasonable place to start, and your child can also book independently — sessions are online, so distance is not an obstacle. Struggling alone overseas is common and rarely mentioned home in full.

How is this different from PGIMER?

Different purpose. PGIMER is a major clinical institution and the right place for psychiatric assessment and serious need. This is non-diagnostic counselling support — conversations for people who are struggling but not ill, without a referral, a waiting list, or a corridor.
